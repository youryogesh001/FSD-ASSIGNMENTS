Operation Safed Sagar was the code name given to the Indian Air Force's (IAF) air campaign during the 1999 Kargil War. Working in close coordination with the Indian Army's ground offensive (Operation Vijay), its primary objective was to flush out Pakistani regular troops and infiltrators who had occupied strategic high-altitude positions along the Line of Control (LoC).

Key Highlights of the Military Operation

• Historical First: It marked the first time the IAF conducted large-scale offensive air strikes at extreme high altitudes (ranging from 16,000 to 23,000 feet).
• Strict Constraints: Ground rules strictly forbade IAF aircraft from crossing the Line of Control into Pakistani territory, meaning pilots had to operate under severe tactical and geographical limitations.
• Fleet Deployed: The IAF deployed a mix of fighter aircraft, including the MiG-21, MiG-23, MiG-27, MiG-29, and the Mirage 2000, alongside Mi-17 helicopters.
• Tactical Innovations: Following initial losses to surface-to-air missiles (SAMs), the IAF shifted strategies. They integrated commercial handheld GPS units into cockpits for medium-altitude blind bombing and deployed Laser-Guided Bombs (LGBs) via Mirage 2000s, successfully destroying major enemy logistical hubs like Muntho Dhalo and turning the tide of the war.
